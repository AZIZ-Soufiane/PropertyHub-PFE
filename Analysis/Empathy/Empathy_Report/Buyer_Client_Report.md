# 🏘️ Report – Interview with End User / Buyer Client: Sarah

**Date:** February 27, 2026  
**Developer:** Soufiane  
**Interviewee:** Sarah  
**Subject:** User Experience and Requirements – "Real Estate Property Search and Purchase Experience"

---

## 1. Introduction

Sarah is a **working professional in her early thirties** who is actively searching for her first property to purchase. During the interview, she shared her expectations, pain points, and behaviors when searching for properties in the real estate market. This report documents her needs as an end-user and buyer, providing valuable insights for designing a user-centric property management system.

---

## 2. User Profile

**Name:** Sarah  
**Age:** 32  
**Occupation:** Software Engineer  
**Location:** Austin, Texas, USA  
**Income Level:** Middle to Upper-Middle Class  
**Experience:** First-time property buyer  

**Context:**  
Sarah has been saving for the past 5 years to purchase her first home in the Austin metropolitan area. She is tech-savvy, prefers online solutions, and values convenience and transparency in the buying process. She has specific location and lifestyle requirements in mind (proximity to tech companies, good schools, walkable neighborhoods) and wants to make an informed decision.

---

## 3. Goals and Needs

### 3.1 Primary Goals

1. **Find the Perfect Property:** Locate a property that matches her requirements (apartment in a safe neighborhood, modern amenities, near workplace)
2. **Save Time:** Minimize time spent browsing through irrelevant listings
3. **Make an Informed Decision:** Have access to comprehensive information before scheduling viewings
4. **Compare Options:** Easily compare multiple properties side by side
5. **Organize Preferences:** Keep track of favorite properties and create a shortlist

### 3.2 Specific Needs

- **Search Functionality:** Quick and intuitive filters (location, price, bedrooms, bathrooms, HOA fees, amenities)
- **Visual Information:** High-quality property images, virtual tours, video walkthroughs, and floor plans
- **Detailed Descriptions:** Comprehensive property information including utilities, property taxes, HOA costs, school district ratings, neighborhood details
- **Location Information:** Easy access to neighborhood maps, nearby amenities, schools, public transportation, commute times to tech hubs
- **Appointment Scheduling:** Seamless booking of property showings without phone calls
- **Communication:** Direct messaging with the real estate agent for clarifications
- **Transparency:** Clear pricing breakdown, no hidden fees, transparent inspection reports and appraisals

---

## 4. Behaviors and Decision-Making Process

### 4.1 Search Behavior

- **Multiple Channels:** Sarah searches on **5-6 different real estate websites and apps** simultaneously
- **Time Investment:** Spends **2-3 hours daily** searching and filtering properties
- **Bookmark Habits:** Saves links in browser bookmarks and Excel spreadsheets to compare properties across platforms
- **Review Patterns:** Reads multiple agent testimonials and verifies property authenticity before contacting

### 4.2 Decision-Making Process

1. **Initial Filtering:** She sets narrow filters based on budget, location, and essential features
2. **Visual Assessment:** She heavily relies on **images and visual content** to eliminate unsuitable properties
3. **Detailed Research:** For properties that pass visual inspection, she researches the neighborhood extensively
4. **Comparison:** She creates spreadsheets comparing 3-5 finalists by price, size, location, amenities
5. **Engagement:** Only after thorough research does she contact the agent to schedule a viewing
6. **Final Decision:** She visits properties, discusses details with the agent, and negotiates terms

---

## 5. Concrete Situations and Pain Points

### 5.1 Frustration 1: Information Scattered Across Multiple Platforms

**Situation:** Sarah found a property she liked on Platform A, but when she wanted to compare it with a similar property on Platform B, there was **no consistent way to organize the information**.
- She had to manually transcribe property details into an Excel file
- Different platforms showed different information about the same property (conflicting prices, descriptions)
- She spent **an entire weekend organizing information** for just 5 properties, which was exhausting and error-prone

**Impact:** She felt frustrated and wasted time that could have been spent visiting properties. She considered stopping her search altogether due to fatigue.

### 5.2 Frustration 2: Poor Quality or Missing Images

**Situation:** A property listing showed only **3 images** despite having 8 rooms. Sarah had to text the agent three times to request more photos.
- When photos were finally sent via text message, they were low resolution and showed poor lighting and angles
- She drove 30 minutes to view the property in person only to discover the interior looked nothing like the photos
- The kitchen appeared dated despite being presented as "recently updated" in the listing

**Impact:** She lost trust in that agent and decided not to pursue the property. She now approaches listings with sparse photos with suspicion and researches the neighborhood on Google Street View to verify authenticity.

### 5.3 Frustration 3: Difficulty Scheduling Viewings

**Situation:** When Sarah found a property she wanted to view, **the only way to schedule a showing was to call during business hours**.
- She works full-time and couldn't always call during agent business hours (typically 9-5 weekdays)
- She missed showing opportunities because she couldn't reach the agent when she called
- When she finally connected with the agent, they weren't available on weekends when she had free time
- She had to coordinate through multiple phone calls and text messages to align on a single showing time

**Impact:** She missed opportunities to view 2 properties that sold to other buyers within 48 hours. She was frustrated with the inflexibility and poor communication experience, especially compared to tech platforms that offer 24/7 booking.

### 5.4 Frustration 4: Lack of Property Comparison Tools

**Situation:** After narrowing down to 4 properties, Sarah wanted to create a **side-by-side comparison** of key features and prices.
- None of the platforms allowed direct comparison
- She had to manually create a spreadsheet with dozens of parameters
- When she found a new property and wanted to add it to her comparison, she had to manually update the spreadsheet
- The process was time-consuming and error-prone

**Impact:** She created comparison documents multiple times and spent excessive time organizing data instead of focusing on making a decision.

### 5.5 Frustration 5: Trust and Authenticity Concerns

**Situation:** Sarah was unsure if properties were **genuinely available and authentic**.
- Some listings remained on websites for months despite being marked as available
- She contacted agents about properties they claimed to have recently listed, only to hear they were sold weeks ago
- Misleading photos made properties look better than they were in reality
- No clear information about property history or previous sale attempts

**Impact:** She lost confidence in several platforms and agents. She spent extra time verifying information through independent research and neighborhood visits.

### 5.6 Frustration 6: Overwhelming Number of Irrelevant Listings

**Situation:** When searching for a 2-bedroom house within her budget in the Austin area, search results displayed **hundreds of listings**, many of which didn't match her criteria.
- Filters didn't work effectively (e.g., "price range" showed properties above her $350K maximum budget)
- She had to manually scroll through pages of irrelevant properties (condos when she wanted houses, properties in the wrong zip code)
- Algorithms didn't seem to understand her preferences (proximity to tech companies, good schools) and kept showing unsuitable options
- Some listings were outdated and showed properties that had already closed

**Impact:** She became overwhelmed and fatigued. She felt she had to spend excessive time filtering manually instead of focusing on relevant opportunities. She nearly gave up on her search.

### 5.7 Frustration 7: No Follow-up or Notification System

**Situation:** Sarah couldn't **set up alerts for new properties matching her criteria**.
- She had to manually check multiple websites daily for new listings
- She missed properties that were listed and sold within hours
- When she scheduled a viewing, she received no confirmation or reminder

**Impact:** She felt she had to be constantly vigilant to not miss opportunities, creating stress and anxiety about the process.

## 6. Expectations for an Ideal System

Based on her experiences, Sarah expects a real estate platform to offer:

1. **Comprehensive Property Information:** High-quality images (minimum 15), detailed descriptions, virtual tours, floor plans
2. **Powerful Search and Filtering:** Accurate filters (price range, bedrooms, bathrooms, lot size, HOA fees, school districts, commute times)
3. **Smart Recommendations:** System learns her preferences and suggests relevant properties in desirable neighborhoods
4. **Comparison Tools:** Built-in tools to compare multiple properties side-by-side with neighborhood data
5. **Easy Scheduling:** Online showing booking with confirmed dates, reminders, and cancellation policies
6. **Transparent Communication:** Direct in-app messaging with agents, guaranteed response times
7. **Verified Listings:** Assurance that listings are current, with last update timestamps and MLS verification
8. **Mobile-Friendly:** Full functionality on phone while commuting or traveling
9. **Favorites and Wishlist:** Save and organize favorite properties with notes about each
10. **Smart Notifications:** Alerts for new properties matching her criteria, price drops on favorites, listing expirations

---

## 7. Behavioral Insights

- Sarah is a **data-driven decision maker** who wants all information accessible before making contact
- She values **time efficiency** and appreciates tools that reduce manual work
- She is **cautious and thorough**, preferring to do extensive research before scheduling viewings
- She is **tech-savvy** and comfortable with modern applications, expecting a smooth digital experience
- She is willing to use **online platforms exclusively** if they provide comprehensive information and reliable tools
- She appreciates **transparency** and concrete information over marketing language

---

## 8. Recommendations for System Design

To meet Sarah's needs:

1. **Invest in Image Quality:** Require high-resolution photos from at least 10 angles for each property
2. **Implement Smart Filtering:** Create accurate, multi-parameter filters that actually work
3. **Build Comparison Tools:** Allow users to select and compare multiple properties side by side
4. **Online Appointment System:** Provide a seamless booking system with confirmations and reminders
5. **Favorites Management:** Simple wishlist feature to save and organize preferred properties
6. **Virtual Tours/Floor Plans:** Provide visual tours or detailed floor plans when available
7. **Transparent Availability:** Clearly show property status (available, pending, sold) with last update information
8. **Direct Communication:** In-app messaging system for users to ask questions without phone calls
9. **Mobile Optimization:** Ensure the platform is fully functional on mobile devices
10. **Notification System:** Alert users about new properties matching their saved preferences

---

## 9. Conclusion

Sarah represents a significant user demographic: **tech-savvy, data-driven buyers** who expect modern, efficient tools for property search and viewing. The real estate platform must not only list properties but provide a comprehensive experience that respects users' time, provides transparency, and enables informed decision-making. By addressing Sarah's pain points—particularly scattered information, appointment scheduling friction, and lack of comparison tools—the system will attract and retain serious buyers like her, ultimately benefiting all stakeholders in the ecosystem.
